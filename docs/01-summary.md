## The idea in one paragraph

Imagine you could buy a shop for less than the value of the stock on its shelves, the cash in its till and the building it sits in, after paying off everything it owes. That would be a bargain on paper. Walter Schloss spent a lifetime looking for the stock-market version of this: companies whose share price is low compared with what the business owns. This project repeats that hunt on three markets and then checks whether each "bargain" is real.

## What we did

1. **Cast a wide net.** Free screening websites listed companies whose share price is well below their "book value" (what the accounts say the company is worth) and that do not owe too much.
2. **Cut the list down.** We removed banks, miners and similar companies, because their accounts need different tools, and kept ordinary operating businesses.
3. **Read the real reports.** For a small group we went to the official filings (the documents companies must publish) and recalculated the key numbers ourselves, instead of trusting the screening website.
4. **Look for the catch.** For each company we asked: why is it cheap, what could go wrong, and are the assets really worth what the accounts say?

## What came out

| Market | Flagged by the screening tool | Examined closely | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| United States | 150 | about 14 | No clean pass. Two names are worth more homework. |
| India (NSE) | 122 | about 5, plus a few skipped for red flags | Three leads, each with real concerns. |
| Australia (ASX) | 78 | 4 in depth, about 20 more checked lightly | Several pass a numbers test, none is a clean pass. |

The counts for "flagged" come from screening tools and have not been individually verified. See [what we found](05-what-we-found.md) for the details.

## Three lessons that matter more than any single company

1. **A cheap ratio is where the work starts, not where it ends.** Several flagged shares failed once we read the filings.
2. **Data vendors make mistakes.** One US company showed a ratio of 0.27 on the screen. From the filing the correct figure is about 1.26. That turns "very cheap" into "not cheap".
3. **The assets have to be real.** Unsold inventory, store leases, fish in a farm and loans owed to others can all make "assets" less valuable than they look.

## What this does not tell you

- It does not tell you what to buy. No company is certified as a buy.
- It is not a complete survey of any market. Most flagged companies were not examined in depth.
- Prices are a snapshot. Some Australian prices have no recorded time and are only indicative.
