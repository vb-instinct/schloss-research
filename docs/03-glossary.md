# Glossary

Plain-English meanings of every term used in this repo.

## Basics

**Share (stock)**: A small slice of ownership in a company.

**Market capitalisation (market cap)**: The price of one share times the number of shares. It is the price tag for the whole company at today's price.

**Exchange**: A market where shares trade. NYSE and Nasdaq are in the US. NSE is the National Stock Exchange of India. ASX is the Australian Securities Exchange.

**Ticker**: The short code for a company on an exchange, such as CATO or IRI.

**Filing**: An official report a listed company must publish (annual report, quarterly report, notices). Filings are the most reliable source for company numbers. The US ones are on the SEC website, Australian ones on the ASX announcements page.

**Screener**: A website where you set filters (for example "price below book value") and get a list of companies. Fast, but the data can be wrong or out of date.

## Value measures

**Book value**: What the company's accounts say the owners' share is worth: everything the company owns minus everything it owes.

**P/B (price to book)**: Market cap divided by book value. Below 1 means the market values the company at less than its accounts do.

**Goodwill and intangibles**: Assets you cannot touch: brand value, software, customer lists, and the extra paid when one company buys another. They can be worth a lot or nothing, so cautious investors strip them out.

**Tangible book value (TBV)**: Book value with goodwill and intangibles removed. A tougher test.

**P/TBV (price to tangible book)**: Market cap divided by tangible book value. This is the main ratio in this repo.

**Current assets**: Cash and things that should turn into cash within a year (money owed by customers, stock to be sold).

**NCAV (net current asset value)**: Current assets minus **all** debts and obligations, not just loans. It asks: if we sold the short-term assets and paid every bill, what is left?

**Net-net**: A share whose market cap is below two-thirds of NCAV. The strictest classic bargain test. Very few companies pass it.

**Margin of safety**: The gap between what you pay and a cautious estimate of what the company is worth. A bigger gap leaves more room for errors.

## Debt and obligations

**Debt-to-equity (D/E)**: Borrowings divided by owners' equity. Under 1 means the company owes less than the owners own.

**Lease liability**: What a company has promised to pay to rent shops, offices or equipment. It is a real obligation even if it is not a bank loan.

**Right-of-use (ROU) asset**: The accounting asset that matches a lease. It is not something you could sell for cash.

**Minority interest (NCI)**: Part of a company's subsidiaries owned by outside investors. Their claim comes before the shareholders you would join.

**Going concern**: A warning from auditors that the company may not be able to keep operating.

**Contingent liability**: A possible future debt, such as a lawsuit or a tax dispute, that may or may not need paying.

## Asset quality

**Inventory**: Stock a company holds for sale. Only worth its book value if it actually sells at that price.

**Receivables**: Money customers owe the company. Some of it may never be paid.

**Impairment**: An accounting write-down when an asset turns out to be worth less than recorded.

**Haircut**: Cutting an asset's value to test the downside, for example "what if inventory is worth 25% less?"

**Deferred tax asset (DTA)**: A possible future tax saving. It only has value if the company makes taxable profits.

## Risks

**Value trap**: A share that looks cheap and stays cheap, or gets cheaper, because the business is genuinely declining.

**Dilution**: The company issues new shares, so each existing share owns a smaller slice.

**Cash burn**: Cash going out faster than it comes in.

**Operating cash flow (OCF)**: Cash a business generates from its normal operations. Negative means it is using cash.

**Reverse split**: A company merges shares (for example 10 into 1) to lift a low share price. It does not change what the company is worth.

**Control or promoter holding**: A single owner or family that holds a large share and can steer the company. Common in India, where owners are called "promoters".
