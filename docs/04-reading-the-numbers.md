# How to read the numbers

[Home](../README.md) | Previous: [Glossary](03-glossary.md) | Next: [What we found](05-what-we-found.md)

Every company in this repo is described with a few ratios. This page explains them with one worked example. Unfamiliar word? See the [glossary](03-glossary.md).

## The three tests we use

| Test | What it measures | Our cut-off | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P/TBV** (price to tangible book) | Price compared with what the company owns, after removing "soft" assets | 0.80 or less for a basic pass, 0.67 or less for the strict tier | Cheaper than 0.67 means you pay under two-thirds of the tangible assets |
| **Debt-to-equity** | How much the company borrowed compared with what owners own | Under 1, and ideally 0.5 or less | High debt can wipe out the owners |
| **Net-net** | Market cap compared with short-term assets after paying **all** obligations | Market cap at or below two-thirds of NCAV | The strictest classic bargain test |

These cut-offs are **our adaptation**. Schloss did not publish a fixed formula (see [his approach](02-schloss-approach.md)).

## A worked example, step by step

Take a made-up company, Example Ltd:

- Shares: 10 million. Share price: $5. So **market cap = $50 million**.
- Owners' equity (book value): $100 million.
- Of that, $20 million is goodwill and other intangibles.

1. **P/B** = $50m divided by $100m = **0.50**. Looks very cheap.
2. **Tangible book value** = $100m minus $20m = **$80m**.
3. **P/TBV** = $50m divided by $80m = **0.63**. Still cheap, and it passes the strict tier (0.67 or less).
4. **Check debt.** Borrowings of $30m divided by equity of $100m gives D/E of 0.30. Fine.
5. **Check NCAV.** Current assets $90m minus all liabilities $60m = $30m. Two-thirds of that is $20m. The market cap of $50m is higher, so Example Ltd is **not a net-net**.
6. **Ask what could be wrong.** Is the $90m of current assets mostly stock that may not sell? A 25% haircut on $60m of inventory removes $15m from the NCAV.

This is the process followed for each real company on [What we found](05-what-we-found.md).

## How to read a ratio

| P/TBV | Rough meaning |
|---|---|
| Above 1.0 | You pay more than the tangible assets. Not a Schloss-style bargain |
| 0.8 to 1.0 | A small discount |
| 0.67 to 0.8 | Basic pass |
| Below 0.67 | Strict tier. Very cheap on paper, which also means the market doubts something |

**A very low ratio is a warning light as much as a green light.** The market is usually pricing in a reason: losses, debts, doubts about the assets. The question is whether the reason is already in the price.

## Why we do not trust one number

- **Screening websites can be wrong.** One company showed P/TBV of 0.27. The filing gives about 1.26.
- **Prices move.** A ratio is only as current as the price behind it.
- **Different markets, different currencies.** US dollars, Australian dollars and Indian rupees are compared within their own market only.
- **Accounts are not cash.** Book value is an accounting number. Real selling prices for factories, stock and shops can be much lower.

## Verdict labels

| Label | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Numeric pass** | Meets the ratio tests on the numbers we used. Nothing more is claimed |
| **Conditional / candidate** | Passes the numbers and deserves more homework, with open concerns |
| **Watch** | Cheap, but with serious doubts or missing information |
| **Excluded / fails** | Does not meet the tests, or something material is wrong |
| **Not diligenced** | Flagged by a screen. We did not read its filings. Not a conclusion either way |

No company in this project has been labelled an unconditional buy.
