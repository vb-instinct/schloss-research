*Snapshot: 13 October 2026. Research only, not advice. Prices and figures will have changed. Terms are in the [glossary](03-glossary.md). Each company links to the filing or page that the numbers came from in the [technical notes](../README.md#for-analysts-the-technical-notes).*

## Overall result

**No company passed every test, so none is certified as a buy.** That is a normal result: a method that only accepts well-supported bargains will often find none. It does not mean there are no bargains in these markets. The search was partial.

## How much was checked

| Market | Flagged by screening tool (unverified) | Checked to varying depth | Not examined |
|---|---|---|---|
| US | 150 strict-tier names | about 14 (balance sheets from SEC filings) | most of the 150 |
| India (NSE) | 122 | about 5, based on a free screening site plus earlier filing work; a few more were set aside after a first look | most of the 122, and only about 20 of 158 rows were visible on the free tool |
| Australia (ASX) | 78 | 4 in depth, others lightly (balance sheets mostly from a data website, not filings) | most of the 78 |

"Not examined" does not mean "rejected" or "approved". It means we do not know.

## United States

| Company | Plain-English verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **HURC** (Hurco, machine tools) | Passes the basic test, not the strict one | Price is about 0.76 of tangible book. No debt and plenty of cash. But a 10% write-down of its stock pushes the ratio to about 0.82. Profit is recovering but uncertain. |
| **CATO** (clothing retailer) | Very cheap on paper, weak protection | Price is a fraction of equity and there is no bank debt. But much of the "asset" backing is store leases, which are not sellable. Lease obligations are large, and recent years include losses. |
| **BOOM** | Fails | The screening site showed 0.27. The filing gives about 1.26. A large claim by outside owners ranks ahead of ordinary shareholders, and cash flow was negative. |
| **MYPS** (PLAYSTUDIOS, mobile games) | Conditional | About 0.60 of tangible book, with cash roughly twice the market value. But revenue and operating results are shrinking, and the share price has had compliance trouble. |
| **DLNG** (Dynagas, LNG ships) | Conditional | Very low ratio, but the ships are about 16 years old, debt is large and its contract income drops after 2028. A foreign filer with a dominant owner. |
| **CAAS** (China Automotive Systems) | Conditional | Low ratio, sales growing. But very large "other" assets appeared, a lot of cash is pledged, and minority owners' claims weaken the net-net test. China-based. |
| HOFT (furniture), BSET (furniture), RELL (electronics), TITN (equipment dealer) | Rejected for now | Either priced above tangible book, or too much debt. |
| MOV (watches), ACNT | Watch, no current price verified | The balance sheet is fine on paper, but the last verified price was well above the cut-off. |
| GENC, LGL | Excluded | Trade on NYSE American (outside the strict list) and fail on price or stale share data. |

Not examined in depth: HOLO, MLGO, AIFC, GDHG, SLMT, SFWL (tiny China-linked listings), PSEC, CLVT, WATR, LNZA.

## India (NSE)

Based on a free screening site's data plus earlier filing work. Only about 20 of 158 rows were visible.

| Company | Plain-English verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Maithan Alloys** | Best asset case, conditional | Price is about 0.63 of tangible book and its investments exceed its market value. But stock sits for over a year, cash flow was negative, and profit swings with investment prices. Not a net-net. |
| **DEN Networks** | Possible net-net on March figures | Cable TV company. But sales are falling, operating cash flow is negative, most profit is "other income", and newer results were not yet seen. |
| **GSFC** (fertiliser) | Watch | Very low ratio and little debt. But cash flow has been negative in four of six years, large government payments are owed to it, and a results meeting was cancelled. |
| **Hinduja Global Solutions** | Not a clean pass | Cheap ratio, but goodwill is unverified, core margins are tiny and there is a quarterly loss. |
| **Orient Paper** | Unverified | Data conflicts and heavy spending on equipment. |

Four other companies were set aside after a first look because of possible warning signs. Those warning signs are unverified: we did not confirm them, and no conclusion about those companies should be drawn from their being set aside. Also skipped: investment holding companies, which need different analysis.

## Australia (ASX)

| Company | Plain-English verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **IRI** (Integrated Research, software) | Strongest of the four checked, still not clean | Cash roughly equals its market value and there is almost no debt. But revenue fell 16%, it made an operating loss, customers are renewing less, and a dividend will reduce the asset backing. |
| **GRR** (Grange Resources, iron ore) | Passes the numbers, risky | Passes the net-net formula. But the company recently took a huge write-down, its mine is ageing, and closure costs are large. Inventory is 45% of its current assets. |
| **CAA** (Capral, aluminium) | Borderline | About 0.78 of tangible book. Low debt and buying back shares. But book value includes A$29m of possible future tax savings, and the industry is cyclical. |
| **OMH** (OM Holdings, manganese) | Not an ordinary pass | Very low ratio, but the headline profit came from selling a business, not ongoing operations. Cash was used up and inventory is three-quarters of current assets. |
| **MCA** (Murray Cod Australia) | Watch | Most of the assets are live fish. It burns far more cash than it earns and has more than doubled its share count in a year. |
| **DBF** (Duxton Farms) | Excluded | Short-term debts exceed short-term assets and there is going-concern doubt. |

Flagged and watched: Shine Justice (client case-work valuation), AAC, Finbar (property developer), Bhagwan Marine (share dilution), Kingston (cash burn), Atlas Pearls (loss-making). Excluded for debt above the cut-off: PWR, EHL, ANG, MHJ, YAL, TBR, JMS, KAR, AEL, FFI. Banks, insurers, property trusts and listed investment companies were not assessed.

**Important:** Australian prices in the diligence came from a data feed with **no time stamp**. They are indicative, not confirmed closing prices.

## What the shortlisted companies have in common

- The cheapest ratios came with the weakest businesses. Greater discount did not mean better value.
- "Assets" often turned out to be leases, stock, fish, investments, or future tax savings, none of which are cash.
- Several companies were shrinking, losing cash or issuing new shares.
