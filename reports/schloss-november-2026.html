The short answer

Nothing became buyable this month. The discounts we track mostly deepened or held, but every name still fails at least one test that matters: assets that shrink or are hard to value, weak cash generation, senior claims ahead of ordinary shareholders, or prices we cannot timestamp. Two names left the list for opposite reasons: BOOM (US) because its price ran 67% away from its assets, and Capral (ASX: CAA) because a 7.6% rise erased its numerical discount. One real development: Grange Resources (ASX: GRR) committed to its underground mine and says existing cash covers it, removing last month's biggest open question on that name - it remains a specialist mining case, not a plain cheap-stock pass.

Coverage and caveats

  • The named list is a selection from hundreds of vendor hits, not a census. Most hits - including new names like WQEY, AIAI, EYPT, ACET, CLW and FBIO in the US and KSN, BRL and LEL on the ASX - were not diligenced this run.
  • 28 US and 11 ASX vendor rows were parked as anomalies (vendor P/B contradicted by computed tangible-book ratios or missing fields) rather than silently deleted.
  • NSE "low-P/B" hits are not tangible-book hits; the free tool cannot strip goodwill or build a true net-net ratio.
  • Prices are vendor quotes without session timestamps; ASX ratios are indicative. Quotes and balance sheets carry different dates - recheck before relying on any number.
  • Financials, miners and holding companies are separated for specialist asset analysis; they are not blanket exclusions.
