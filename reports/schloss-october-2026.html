Framework and criteria

Walter Schloss: framework and implementation

13 October 2026. Research, not personalized financial advice. No trades have been placed.

What the complete library changes

Schloss is an asset-discount investor, not a low-P/E stock picker and not a concentrated quality-growth investor. He starts with today's assets and price, checks the liabilities, company history, cash flow, ownership and downside, and accepts an uncertain recovery timetable. A cheap ratio opens a file; it does not close the investment decision.

All 33 linked PDFs, 182 pages, were retrieved and read. Image scans required OCR; the 47-year results table and all six pages of the weak industrial-average scan were inspected visually. Two Graham reminiscence files duplicate the same essay. The 65-page item titled Part I includes Part II. The video entry is a written summary of a talk, not the original recording. The library provides a three-page excerpt of Financial Intelligence Report, not the seven-page issue listed in its contents. Every supplied article is covered; this does not claim every underlying publication or linked video was reviewed.

Original principles
  1. Relate price to conservative intrinsic worth, not merely to another expensive stock. A share is ownership of a business.
  2. Start with book and tangible assets. The original November 1993 Columbia checklist explicitly asks whether goodwill is in book. Assets are generally less volatile than earnings, but can still be impaired.
  3. Prefer little or no debt. The March 1994 memo says debt should not equal 100% of equity. Bankruptcy, leases, pensions and obligations can defeat apparently cheap assets.
  4. Investigate why a price is depressed. Review long price history, company history, cash flow, income, margins, competitors, insider ownership and downside versus upside. A multi-year low is a lead, not proof of value.
  5. Read annual reports, quarterly reports, proxies and footnotes. Look for honest, shareholder-oriented management, not persuasive presentations. Insider ownership can align interests but cannot guarantee minority shareholders receive value.
  6. Diversify because individual outcomes are uncertain. His talks describe dozens to roughly 100 holdings, not a three-stock conviction portfolio. Small initial positions and room to add avoided overconcentration.
  7. Be patient. An average four-year holding period is historical experience, not a promised exit deadline. Monthly research does not mean monthly portfolio turnover.
  8. Reassess on changed facts. Scale buying only when value remains intact, not automatically because price falls. Intertan bought around half book and sold at a loss is a documented value-trap example.
  9. Sell when the price becomes reasonable against revised value. The original 1994 memo explicitly warns against automatically selling simply because a stock has risen 50%.
  10. Keep costs and emotions low. Avoid investment leverage, tips, imitation and macroeconomic forecasts as the basis for a purchase.
What is not an original universal rule

The numerical thresholds below are a reproducible conservative adaptation. Schloss evolved from net-nets to half/two-thirds of book, then sometimes book or a small premium and special situations. He did not publish one invariant global screener.

The AAII May 2009 reconstruction uses price below book, price within 10% of a 52-week low, insider ownership above the database median, zero long-term debt in both annual and quarterly reports, a seven-year price-history proxy, exclusion of ADRs and financials. It calls these its translation of his approach. Do not present them as his precise rules. It also treats book as liquidation value too confidently; asset realizability, liquidation costs and minority rights control actual proceeds.

Financials and miners are not blanket Schloss prohibitions: the corpus contains ASARCO, Cleveland Cliffs and insurers. They are excluded from this simple operating-company screen because their assets require different analysis. He generally preferred US companies and worried about foreign accounting/politics; British exceptions and foreign holdings appear in the corpus. ASX and NSE are adaptations, not markets Schloss endorsed. The OID interview explicitly excludes tobacco for his own ethical reasons.

Reproducible monthly screen
  • Scope: listed ordinary/common operating-company equities on ASX, US exchanges and NSE. Record the exact exchanges and universe actually accessed. US first pass reviewed NYSE/Nasdaq; NYSE American names were separately noted, not rejected as a Schloss rule.
  • Discovery: P/book <1, positive equity, low debt, established operating history. Prefer 20-year businesses; investigate at least 10 years where available. Do not silently exclude newer businesses without documenting the choice.
  • Baseline asset screen: price/tangible common book <=0.80. Strict asset tier <=0.67. These are implementation thresholds. Tangible common equity = equity attributable to ordinary owners minus goodwill and other intangibles; deduct preferred interests where applicable.
  • Leverage: financial debt/common equity <1, preferably <=0.5 or genuine net cash. Show leases separately and alongside debt; review liquidity and covenants. Do not label investment securities as cash or count blocked facilities as liquidity.
  • Net-net: NCAV = current assets minus ALL liabilities and any senior claims not already deducted. Candidate when market capitalization <=2/3 NCAV. Avoid the common shortcut subtracting only borrowings.
  • Denominator: market cap uses latest shares including all economic common classes. Per-share valuation uses a conservative current fully diluted count. Reconcile weighted-average EPS shares against issued shares, rights issues, options and post-report corporate actions. Do not combine pre-issue equity with post-issue shares.
  • Price: latest completed regular exchange session, exchange/date/time/currency/source. Mark an undated quote undated. Never certify a current pass with weeks-old quotes. Recheck immediately before any investment decision.
  • Quality gates: inventories/receivables, deferred-tax assets, investments, ROU assets, property revaluations, restricted cash, contingent claims, pensions, closure liabilities, capex and ongoing losses. Separate audited book from a conservative realizable-value stress test.
  • Governance: control, related-party transactions, dilution, dividends/buybacks and whether assets can ever benefit minority owners. No unsupported takeover/catalyst claims.
  • Price history: verify 2-3-year and ideally 10-year lows/highs; 52-week proximity is a secondary signal, not a substitute. First market reports did not fully verify multi-year histories.
  • Verdict: numeric pass, conditional diligence candidate, watch, excluded or unverified. Buy-worthy requires price, latest financials and stressed asset protection all supported; no fixed quota of buys.
Ranking and investment use

Prioritize durable asset protection, low obligations, cash-burn resilience and governance before size of the headline discount. Do not translate a ratio into a return forecast. What the free tiers could not do
  • Screener.in free: no CSV export (premium, Rs.4,999/yr - confirmed by hitting the premium gate on Download Results); no industry column in screen results; custom-ratio formula builder rejects balance-sheet liability terms ('Total liabilities', 'Borrowings', 'Other liabilities' all rejected - only 'Current assets' and 'Debt' accepted), so a true NCAV/net-net ratio cannot be built; P/B is book (not tangible book) - goodwill/intangibles stripping happens in diligence; no quote timestamps.
  • TradingView free: scanner fundamentals are fiscal-quarter vendor data without date stamps; India coverage is materially incomplete (P/B, D/E, balance-sheet fields are null for GSFC, Maithan, DEN and most NSE names - that is why NSE runs on Screener.in instead); no native tangible-book ratio (computed from equity - goodwill - intangibles in the screen calculation); saved-screen and layout limits on Basic plan not yet probed; ads.
  • Both: no 10-20-year operating-history filter, no 'why is it cheap' data, no lease/deferred-tax/cash-burn adjustments. Those stay in the per-name diligence step, unchanged from the current framework.
