# Screening rules

> **Technical note.** For a plain explanation of each rule, see [How to read the numbers](../docs/04-reading-the-numbers.md) and the [glossary](../docs/03-glossary.md).

These are the rules the screens used. They are our own cautious adaptation of Walter Schloss's approach. He did not publish one fixed formula.

| Rule | Setting |
|---|---|
| Companies | Ordinary operating businesses on US exchanges, NSE (India) and ASX (Australia). Banks, insurers, property trusts, investment holding companies and miners are separated out for different analysis |
| Base screen | Price to book value (P/B) at most 0.8, debt-to-equity at most 1, positive book value |
| Basic asset tier | Price to tangible book value (P/TBV) at most 0.80 |
| Strict asset tier | P/TBV at most 0.67 |
| Net-net | Market cap at most two-thirds of net current asset value (NCAV), where NCAV is current assets minus **all** liabilities |
| Debt | Borrowings below owners' equity, ideally at most half. Leases shown separately |
| Size gate | US: market cap at least US$30m. ASX: at least A$30m (and A$15m on one secondary list). India: at least Rs250 crore |
| Price | Latest completed regular trading session, dated. Undated quotes are labelled as such |
| Diligence | A screen hit is a lead. Check filings for tangible assets, leases, inventory, receivables, cash burn, share count changes, ownership and why the price is low |
| Verdicts | Numeric pass, conditional, watch, excluded, or not diligenced. No fixed number of buys per month |
