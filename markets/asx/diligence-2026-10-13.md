# ASX strict-tier fundamentals diligence

> **Technical note for analysts.** Dense working notes with shorthand. New here? Start with the [plain-English summary](../../docs/01-summary.md) and [what we found](../../docs/05-what-we-found.md). Figures are a snapshot from 13 October 2026. Research only, not advice.

Research date: Tuesday, 13 October 2026. Research only, not a buy recommendation.

## Bottom line

Of four names checked, **Integrated Research (IRI) is the strongest candidate for further work**, because it has cash, little financial leverage and positive cash generation. But shrinking renewals, a large new bad-debt provision, and a dividend that reduces asset backing stop it being a clean pass. **OM Holdings (OMH) belongs in a separate commodity/smelter bucket**, not the ordinary ex-miners shortlist. **Murray Cod Australia (MCA) is a fish-inventory and funding story**, not a cash-backed bargain. **Duxton Farms (DBF) is a financing/asset-sale special situation with going-concern uncertainty**. The biggest discounts did not produce the best fundamentals.

Scope: IRI from the prior shortlist plus the three lowest reported P/TBV names in the rebuilt ASX list (OMH, MCA, DBF). This is not diligence on all 78 vendor-data strict-tier hits. Sector labels in that screen are not investment classifications.

For comparability: tangible equity means consolidated equity attributable to ordinary owners less goodwill and all identifiable intangibles. NCAV means current assets minus **all** liabilities, including leases, deferred tax, provisions and deferred revenue. No liability is forgiven because it may not need immediate cash payment. ## 1. Integrated Research: strongest balance sheet, deteriorating core business

**Business/history.** Founded in 1988, providing Prognosis monitoring/diagnostics for business-critical computing, payments and communications across more than 40 countries. Established customers and recurring maintenance are positives. But it is not a high-retention SaaS growth business: Collaborate's FY26 constant-currency net revenue retention was only 83%, unchanged from FY25. Transact's 18% growth was helped by early multi-year renewals, so do not extrapolate it without contract-cohort work.

**Fundamentals.** FY26 revenue A$57.638m, down 16% from A$68.256m. Collaborate down 27%; Infrastructure down 19%; Transact up 18%. Statutory EBITDA negative A$2.136m (about -3.7% of revenue), versus positive A$15.882m FY25. Reported NPAT A$1.210m is less reassuring than it looks: finance income and a tax benefit help bridge an operating loss. Total contract value fell A$83.869m FY24 -> A$67.273m FY25 -> A$54.317m FY26. Management's pro forma EBITDA A$8.666m is not substituted for the statutory result.

**Cash.** Operating cash flow A$12.648m, versus A$8.679m FY25. Capex only A$0.257m, so operating cash flow less equipment capex is A$12.391m before lease principal and financing/distributions. This does not prove current sales are profitable: collections of earlier licences and changes in receivables affect conversion. Cash A$51.693m exceeds the indicative A$50.157m market cap, but it is not distributable surplus free of obligations.

**Assets.** Equity A$98.021m; no capitalised intangible asset balance in the June statement. Current assets A$91.148m less all liabilities A$21.359m = NCAV A$69.789m. Two-thirds is A$46.526m, below the indicative market cap. Debt is essentially leases A$1.521m, not bank borrowings. Receivables are material: current A$35.900m plus non-current A$22.343m. The A$4.978m new expected-credit-loss charge includes a single-client A$3.8m provision. Remaining current receivables deserve scrutiny.

**Dividend/dilution.** A 5c dividend was declared after balance date, record date 3 September, payable 15 October. A current buyer should not add that dividend to the ex-entitlement price or count the June cash before it leaves. Using the latest quoted share count of 182,388,666 from the 30 September Appendix 2A gives an approximate A$9.119m payout adjustment: TBV A$88.902m, NCAV A$60.670m. The exact entitlement share count could differ slightly. The share count increased by 1,796,863 on 30 September, so the earlier 180.592m balance-date count is not used for current market cap. October 13 cessation notice is listed, but no claim of complete option reconciliation is made.

**Ownership/history.** Directors named in the August report own 701,263 ordinary shares in aggregate (about 0.38% of the September count); CEO Ian Lowe had no ordinary shares and 984,621 performance rights. This is not large founder alignment. A primary FY2016 report shows revenue A$84.532m and NPAT about A$16m. The much lower FY26 revenue/profit means long existence alone is not business durability. This is a ten-year endpoint comparison, not a complete annual ten-year series.

**Why cheap, downside/upside.** Weak renewals, lower contract values, procurement delays and bad debt provide fundamental reasons for the discount. Software know-how is not liquidation collateral. Post-dividend NCAV under a 25% haircut to current receivables falls to about A$51.695m; a 50% haircut falls to A$42.720m, below current indicative market cap. Extra cash burn further reduces protection. Upside would require renewed contracts, collections and disciplined capital returns, not merely a rerating to June book. Before upgrading: verify fresh close, exact dividend payout, remaining receivable quality and FY27 renewal evidence.

## 2. OM Holdings: deep discount, but recurring economics worse than headline profit

**Business/history.** Long-standing manganese/ferroalloy group with mining history, trading and capital-intensive smelters, including Malaysia. Founder/executive chair Low Ngee Tong has led the business since listing in 1998. This is commodity cycle exposure, not an ordinary industrial liquidation case.

**Fundamentals.** H1 2026 revenue US$265.234m, down 14.2%. Gross margin improved to 11.4% from about 7.0%, but continuing operations lost US$24.052m after tax. The headline US$26.353m profit incorporates US$50.405m profit from discontinued operations. It is not proof the continuing smelting business recovered. Operating cash outflow was US$72.4m; inventory rose to US$299.112m. Borrowings US$181.094m plus leases US$3.368m are about 41% of parent equity. Cash excluding collateral is US$38.153m; US$10.683m collateral is not freely available cash.

**Assets/NCAV.** Parent equity US$452.069m (not total equity US$454.515m including minority interests). No goodwill balance is shown. Some land-use/exploration balances warrant specialist haircut, but even stripping them does not erase the headline discount. Current assets US$391.100m less ALL liabilities US$336.130m = US$54.970m NCAV. Indicative market cap A$153.251m substantially exceeds two-thirds of converted NCAV (about A$52.646m). Inventory is 76.5% of current assets. A 25% inventory haircut makes NCAV negative US$19.808m. Smelting plant resale value, financing claims and inventory realisability matter more than the 0.24 ratio.

**Ownership/history.** April 2026 substantial-holder register: Low Ngee Tong 9.02%; Huang Gang 13.52%; Amplewood Resources 13.08%; Heng Siow Kwee 8.77%. Nominee holdings are not independently treated as beneficial ownership. FY2016 primary report: revenue A$414.2m, net loss A$16.7m, operating cash inflow A$22.3m. FY2021-25 parent profits in the latest annual report: US$61.5m, US$67.8m, US$18.1m, US$9.3m, US$2.3m. Currency/presentation differs across the decade; these are evidence of cyclicality, not a like-for-like growth series.

**Why cheap, downside/upside.** Oversupply, subdued steel/alloy demand, thin margins and working-capital absorption explain the discount. Asset disposal has helped deleveraging, but is not repeatable earnings. Upside is continuing margin recovery and inventory-to-cash conversion. Downside is an extended commodity trough, specialised-plant impairments and further capital needs. A 15 October special meeting proposes Bermuda-to-Singapore redomiciliation and name change to OM Materials Holdings; not yet treated as approved. Keep in specialist watch, not a screen pass.

Sources: H1 report [S6], annual 2025 [S7], annual 2016 [S8], meeting [S9], FX [S10]. The H1 balance sheet and income statement were visually checked to correct OCR.

## 3. Murray Cod Australia: book value mainly fish, cash burn far larger than sales

**Business/history.** Aquna-branded farmed Murray cod, hatcheries and processing. January 2017 acquisition anchors the current operating business. This does not meet a verified 10-20-year record for today's business, even though its listed corporate shell is older.

**Fundamentals.** FY26 sales revenue A$13.493m, up 24%, but loss A$5.370m versus A$8.558m profit FY25. The income statement includes A$29.540m fair-value biological gains and a A$2.244m negative valuation adjustment. These dwarf cash sales. Fish cost of sales A$24.069m exceeds revenue; with fair-value inventory accounting, this is not a clean conventional gross-margin measure. Underlying unit profitability is not established from the published totals. Operating cash outflow A$26.573m versus A$16.907m FY25; capex A$2.072m. A$35.590m gross equity issuance funded the year. Do not call it self-funding.

**Assets/NCAV.** Equity A$125.149m less ALL intangibles A$5.855m = TBV A$119.294m. Intangibles include A$3.614m tradable water rights, which could retain value in a specialist appraisal; this conservative calculation strips them regardless. Current assets A$91.171m less all liabilities A$49.683m = NCAV A$41.488m. Two-thirds A$27.659m is below the A$34.702m indicative market cap. Inventory A$86.143m is 94.5% of current assets, including net biological fish A$84.151m. A 25% inventory haircut leaves NCAV only A$19.953m; 50% makes it slightly negative. Disease, oxygen failure, feed costs, harvest timing, market price and unaccounted fish are real risks, not minor footnotes. June cash was A$4.145m; a July R&D refund A$3.432m improves liquidity but does not cure recurring burn.

**Dilution/ownership.** Shares grew from 105.775m after the prior consolidation to 247.871m by June 2026, about 134% growth in one year. Chairman Brett Paton and associates 24.463m shares, about 9.87%. FY26 KMP table totals 47.239m shares (19.1%), but includes people who left during the year, so it is not a current director-ownership percentage. Current CEO Steven Chaur bought 250,000 shares at A$0.16 on 29 September, increasing his disclosed indirect holding to 513,157. Alignment is a positive; it does not make live fish cash-equivalent.

**Why cheap, downside/upside.** Growth and commercial sales-channel expansion might turn inventory into cash, but the gap between biomass values and realised sales is large. The company can look statistically cheap while every share loses backing through losses and discounted issuance. Keep on watch only until harvest/receipts, cash gross profit and cash burn improve materially.

Sources: FY26 report [S11], current CEO ownership filing [S12], 2022 report/history [S13]. A complete annual ten-year cash-flow series is not established.

## 4. Duxton Farms: property discount, but financing pressure dominates

**Business/history.** Listed in 2018 as a broadacre farm owner. A 2025 merger added four private agricultural businesses spanning horticulture, viticulture and apiculture. A new post-merger portfolio cannot be treated as a seasoned ten-year operating business. Drought/flood cycles, property sales and a development pivot limit historical comparability.

**Fundamentals.** FY26 revenue A$18.114m; gross loss A$4.165m (about -23%). Net loss A$40.324m; underlying EBITDA loss A$12.921m. Operating cash outflow A$30.759m, versus A$6.603m FY25. Business-combination expenses A$6.912m explain part, not all, of the loss. Cash A$1.217m against overdraft A$14.482m. No clean defensive earnings stream.

**Assets/obligations.** Equity A$115.080m less intangibles A$11.690m = TBV A$103.390m. Current assets A$100.979m less all liabilities A$132.630m = NEGATIVE A$31.651m NCAV. A$74.9m of current assets is property/disposal groups held for sale, not operating working capital. Borrowings including overdraft A$103.924m; leases A$6.592m. Debt plus leases is about 96% of book equity and 107% of tangible equity. The apparent 0.36 P/TBV coexists with much higher balance-sheet risk than the simple screen suggests.

**Going concern and financing.** Primary annual report explicitly describes dependence on Forbes aggregation sale, other divestments, capex cuts, debt extensions and/or fresh equity/convertible notes. On 1 October the company reported A$4.807m notes issued, plus shortfall commitments bringing the headline to A$6.566m only INCLUDING amounts requiring shareholder approval. This is not the full roughly A$9.74m originally sought. Notes offer 7.5% cash coupon or 12% capitalised return and conversion, introducing claims ahead of ordinary equity and dilution risk. Do not say all funding is secured.

**Ownership/governance.** Chairman Ed Peter has substantial disclosed direct/indirect interests; the annual report's director table lists 17.427m ordinary shares (about 16% of June shares). The October filing reports note participation, not a new on-market equity purchase. Management/service fees A$1.936m and connected-party relationships need review. High insider exposure can align incentives but also creates conflicts; nominee names are not assumed to be owners.

**Why cheap, downside/upside.** Development assets and land might realise book values over time. But funding distress can force discounted sales or highly dilutive capital. FY26 shares rose from 41.157m to 107.118m, largely merger consideration, so old per-share NAV is not comparable. A 20% haircut just to the A$74.9m assets held for sale removes A$14.98m from equity, before ongoing burn, financing and sale costs. Recovery requires completed disposals near carrying values, financing and a credible route to cash profit. Exclude from the conservative list; retain only as a high-risk special situation if explicitly wanted.

Sources: FY26 annual [S14], 1 October notes result [S15], October director-interest filing [S16], independent agricultural context [S17].

## What would change the ranking

IRI: renewed sales/retention, collections, conservative post-dividend asset coverage. OMH: continuing smelter profitability and working capital release. MCA: cash sale volumes/profit catching up with biomass, lower burn without recurrent dilution. DBF: settled sales and funding, not merely planned disposals or conditional commitments. No price-to-book discount is a promised return or a liquidation price target.

Open gaps: certified latest-session closes for all four; full audited annual ten-year series; complete subsequent-announcement/ownership and dilution reconciliation; executable liquidation values and transaction costs. Primary reports provide substantial evidence but cannot solve those questions alone. No verified ASX buy emerges from these four.
